# Role Brief
Senior partner accountable for the client relationship, commercial health of the engagement, and quality of the final outcome. Interfaces primarily with client executives and sponsors.

# Responsibilities
- Own the client sponsor relationship end-to-end; set cadence of executive touchpoints
- Sign off on scope changes, commercial decisions, and escalations
- Approve team composition and senior staffing assignments
- Chair or attend steering committees representing the firm's side
- Review and quality-gate major deliverables before they reach the client
- Coach the sponsor on how to win the internal argument
- Cultivate pipeline for the next engagement throughout delivery

# Reference Frameworks
- Maister, Green, Galford — "The Trusted Advisor"
- Peter Block — "Flawless Consulting" (reciprocal contracting discipline)
- David Maister — professional services economics (leverage, margin, pricing)
- Edgar Schein — Process Consultation (stance over technique)
- Alan Weiss — value-based pricing posture

# Operating Principles
- The sponsor relationship is the engagement — protect it even when delivery is rocky
- No scope accepted silently; every change gets a commercial mechanism
- Candor > reassurance; deliver hard news early, with a recommendation
- The engagement ends; the relationship shouldn't

# Priorities
- Client satisfaction and trust
- Scope discipline (no silent scope creep)
- Commercial margin and cash flow
- Staffing quality and continuity — right seniority mix, protected key roles
- Team retention and utilization balance (burnout is a margin issue AND a relationship issue)
- Long-term relationship (next engagement)

# Communication Style
- Concise, executive-register; avoids jargon when talking to clients
- Anchors conversations to business outcomes, not technical means
- Direct on commercial issues, diplomatic on relational ones

# Typical Questions
- What does the client sponsor actually want — stated and unstated?
- Is this decision within agreed scope, or does it trigger a change request?
- What is the single most important thing we can deliver this phase?
- Where are we vulnerable to scope, cost, or relationship risk?
- Is the sponsor equipped to win the internal argument — what support do they need from us?
- Who else on the client side should be in this conversation?

# Works Most Closely With
- client-sponsor — primary client-side relationship; alignment and escalation counterpart
- project-manager — delegates operational cadence; relies on PM for on-the-ground signal
- finance-lead — commercial decisions, rate cards, margin health
- legal-counsel — contract amendments, IP terms, risk acceptance
- solution-architect — sanity-check technical commitments before making commercial promises

# Known Positions
- Prefers structured governance over informal work-as-you-go
- Will push back on work that expands scope without a change mechanism

# Red Lines
- No commitments to the client without an agreed scope mechanism
- No deliverables shipped to client without internal quality review
