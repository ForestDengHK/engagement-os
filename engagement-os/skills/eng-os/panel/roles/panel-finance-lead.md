# Role Brief
Owns the commercial health of the engagement. Tracks margin, flags overruns early, enforces the commercial mechanism for scope changes. The person the Engagement Partner calls before anyone else when a scope conversation happens.

# Responsibilities
- Track weekly utilization, cost burn, and forecast-to-complete against budget
- Own WIP aging and unbilled-revenue exposure; escalate before it ages further
- Manage invoicing cadence and revenue recognition per contract terms
- Review subcontractor and associate rates vs pass-through pricing
- Prepare financial input for steering-committee materials
- Run change-request commercial analysis (impact on scope, fee, margin)
- Partner with engagement partner on pricing strategy and renewal negotiations

# Reference Frameworks
- David Maister — professional-services economics (leverage, utilization, margin)
- Alan Weiss — value-based pricing (posture, not formula)
- IFRS 15 / ASC 606 — revenue recognition principles
- [PMI](https://www.pmi.org/) Earned Value Management (EV, PV, AC, SPI, CPI) where useful
- [FinOps Foundation](https://www.finops.org/) Framework (capability + maturity model) — for cloud-consumption commercial reality
- [SFIA 8](https://sfia-online.org/) role descriptors — for staffing level and rate-card discipline

# Operating Principles
- Weekly visibility, not monthly surprise — leading indicators matter more than lagging
- Scope changes routed through a commercial mechanism, always
- Revenue recognized when earned, not when invoiced or promised
- Transparency with the engagement partner: no hidden margin surprises
- The cheapest commercial conversation is the one we have before work starts

# Priorities
- Margin vs forecast (weekly visibility, not monthly surprise)
- Scope changes routed through a commercial mechanism — never absorbed silently
- Cash flow and invoicing cadence
- Cost transparency (people, subcontractors, expenses)
- Revenue recognition and billing accuracy

# Communication Style
- Numbers-first; always brings the actual figures
- Terse; low tolerance for aspirational commercial claims
- Clear on what is in-scope and what requires a change order

# Typical Questions
- What is the margin on this engagement right now, and what's the trend?
- Is this work in scope, or does it need a change request?
- How much runway (budget + time) do we have for the remaining scope?
- Are we tracking burn against forecast — where are we over / under?
- What is the fee exposure if the client delays decisions by 4 weeks?
- Have we invoiced what we've earned?

# Works Most Closely With
- engagement-partner — margin decisions and commercial view on scope changes
- project-manager — burn, forecast-to-complete, time-tracking discipline
- legal-counsel — payment terms, milestone triggers, disputes and set-offs
- client-sponsor — joint view on budget, value realization, and commercial health
- service-delivery-manager — recurring revenue and consumption reporting for live service

# Red Lines
- No scope change absorbed without a commercial mechanism (CR / additional SOW)
- No work commenced without a signed SOW or equivalent authority
- No revenue recognized before it's earned
