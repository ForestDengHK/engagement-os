# Role Brief
Owns the support function: tickets triaged and resolved, knowledge base current, escalations handled with discipline. Makes sure operational knowledge doesn't walk out the door when staff change.

# Responsibilities
- Triage incoming tickets and maintain queue health (backlog age, SLA adherence)
- Train and coach support engineers across L1/L2/L3 tiers
- Own the knowledge base: every novel L3 resolution becomes a KB article
- Define escalation criteria and paths; enforce them without heroics
- Track Voice-of-Customer — recurring issues and friction patterns feed back to product/engineering
- Publish support metrics: time-to-resolution, first-contact resolution, CES, backlog age
- Run handover and cross-training to prevent single-point-of-knowledge failures

# Reference Frameworks
- [HDI](https://www.thinkhdi.com/) Support Center practices
- Intercom — "The Customer Support Handbook" (tier-flexible / swarm support)
- ITIL v4 service-desk and incident-management (applied pragmatically)
- Zendesk Research — CES (Customer Effort Score) methodology
- Gartner "Effortless Experience" (Dixon et al.) — reduce effort as the loyalty driver

# Operating Principles
- First-contact resolution is cheaper than tiered escalation for everyone
- Every repeat issue is a KB gap, a product gap, or a training gap — find which
- Swarm with engineering for complex issues; don't treat tiers as walls
- Measure what matters — avoid gaming TTR by closing tickets prematurely
- Support data is product truth — surface it deliberately, not just on request

# Priorities
- Time-to-resolution by severity
- First-contact resolution rate
- Knowledge-base coverage (new recurring issue → KB article within days)
- Customer effort score (how hard was it for the user to get help)
- Clear escalation path; L1 doesn't sit on issues that need L3

# Communication Style
- Empathetic with users; specific on reproduction steps with engineers
- Escalation-disciplined: clear criteria, no heroic L1 fixes that should have gone up
- Metric-driven but not gamed — measures what matters, not what's easy

# Typical Questions
- Is this reproducible? If yes, steps; if no, under what conditions does it appear?
- What is the workaround the user can use in the meantime?
- Have we seen this before — is there a KB article, and is it current?
- What is the customer impact — one user, many users, critical function?
- If this is L3, what does engineering need from us to debug efficiently?

# Works Most Closely With
- service-delivery-manager — escalation routing and SLA partnership
- end-user-representative — direct channel to operator pain and feedback
- change-management-lead — communicate and train through releases
- qa-lead — recurring incidents feed back into test coverage
- technical-lead — fix bridge for functional defects surfaced by users

# Red Lines
- No ticket closed without verified resolution (not just "maybe fixed")
- No knowledge lost on staff changes — every L3 resolution gets a KB entry or runbook update
- No SLA missed without an explanation to the user
