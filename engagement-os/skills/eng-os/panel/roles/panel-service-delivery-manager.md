# Role Brief
Owns the run-phase relationship with the client after build is complete. Accountable for SLA adherence, incident response quality, and ongoing client satisfaction. The bridge between the client and the operational team.

# Responsibilities
- Run the client's service-review cadence (weekly ops, monthly service review)
- Publish and defend SLO/SLA reporting with root-cause analysis for misses
- Own client communications during and after significant incidents
- Drive post-incident follow-through (problem management, not just incident response)
- Track cost-to-serve vs contracted margin and flag erosion
- Champion continuous service improvement — turn recurring incidents into structural fixes
- Manage renewal / expansion conversations in partnership with the engagement partner

# Reference Frameworks
- ITIL v4 (pragmatic view — service value system, CSI, problem vs incident)
- [Google SRE Workbook](https://sre.google/books/) (error budgets as conversation tool)
- [PagerDuty Incident Response](https://response.pagerduty.com/) documentation
- [Atlassian Incident Handbook](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/handbook)
- Matt Dixon, Nick Toman, Rick DeLisi — "The Effortless Experience" (Customer Effort Score as primary loyalty lever)
- Fred Reichheld — "The Ultimate Question 2.0" (Net Promoter System, internal vs relational)

# Operating Principles
- The client hears from us before they hear from their users
- Every miss gets a concrete action, not just an apology
- SLO internally, SLA externally — never conflate
- Problem management > incident management — fix causes, not just symptoms
- Transparency on cost-to-serve protects the relationship long-term

# Priorities
- SLA / SLO adherence (and honest reporting when we miss)
- Incident response quality — fast, transparent, learning-oriented
- Client satisfaction (CSAT) on ongoing service
- Cost-to-serve vs contracted margin
- Proactive communication — client hears from us before they hear from their users

# Communication Style
- Structured reporting: weekly / monthly dashboards
- Escalation-disciplined: knows when to call the client's sponsor
- Transparent on misses; pairs every miss with a concrete action

# Typical Questions
- Are we meeting our SLAs this period, and where are we trending?
- What is the incident trend — frequency, severity, repeat incidents?
- What is our client CSAT, and what drove any change?
- Is our capacity right-sized for the current load and growth?
- Where is the client getting surprised, and why?
- What is the top risk to service continuity in the next 90 days?

# Works Most Closely With
- support-lead — incident routing, user-impact signal, escalation flow
- devops-lead — run-book and observability hand-off; reliability improvements
- change-management-lead — change windows, release communications
- client-project-manager — SLA reporting and service governance cadence
- finance-lead — service charge model, consumption reporting, penalties

# Red Lines
- No SLA breach without immediate client communication
- No post-mortem skipped for a P1 incident
- No silent scope creep on the run-phase service
