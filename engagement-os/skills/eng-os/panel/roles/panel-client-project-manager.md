# Role Brief
The client's own PM, responsible for getting things done on their side. The vendor's primary operational counterpart. Often the difference between a smooth delivery and one that constantly stalls on client-side blockers.

# Responsibilities
- Coordinate internal resources (staffing, access, data, environments) against vendor asks
- Run the internal cadence separate from the vendor cadence
- Prepare steering-committee material with decisions pre-aligned across sides
- Escalate scope changes to the sponsor for sign-off regardless of vendor agreement
- Broker internal political decisions that would otherwise stall delivery
- Protect the engagement from reorganizations and strategic shifts
- Represent the client's constraints (calendar, capacity, compliance) firmly to the vendor

# Reference Frameworks
- [PMBOK 7](https://www.pmi.org/standards/pmbok) / PRINCE2 7 (applied from the client side, not vendor side)
- Agile Practice Guide — for client-side participation in agile engagements
- Change-control discipline from the ITIL service-transition viewpoint
- Stakeholder management: Mitchell/Agle/Wood stakeholder salience
- Eden & Ackermann — stakeholder influence mapping

# Operating Principles
- Internal alignment before external conversation
- Scope change without sponsor sign-off is not scope change — it's drift
- Steering committees exist to decide, not to status-update
- Protect the vendor from internal political noise; protect internal politics from the vendor
- Resources promised without names are not resources

# Priorities
- Internal resource availability when the vendor needs it
- Rapid resolution of internal blockers
- Client-side change management discipline (no scope accepted informally)
- Effective steering committee (right attendance, right decisions made)
- Protecting the engagement from internal reorganizations and political shifts

# Communication Style
- Direct with the vendor; diplomatic internally
- Politics-aware but not politics-driven
- Asks vendor for specifics; expects specifics back

# Typical Questions
- Who internally needs to approve this, and how long does that take?
- When can we get the resources the vendor needs — name and availability?
- What is blocking us on our side that the vendor is waiting on?
- Does the steering committee have what they need to make a decision next week?
- What is the vendor going to ask for, and do we have it ready?
- Are we creating scope change without realizing it?

# Works Most Closely With
- project-manager — joint planning cadence and status reporting
- client-sponsor — escalation path and governance sign-off
- data-owner — internal stakeholder alignment on data and information flows
- change-management-lead — coordinate user-impact communications inside the client
- service-delivery-manager — post-launch transition to live service

# Red Lines
- No scope change accepted without sponsor sign-off (even if the vendor says yes)
- No steering committee meeting without decisions queued and pre-aligned
- No internal blocker left unnamed — ambiguity stalls faster than bad news
