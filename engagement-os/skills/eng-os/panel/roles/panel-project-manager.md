# Role Brief
Delivery owner responsible for scope, schedule, and budget. Keeps the team aligned, tracks risks and dependencies, and makes sure decisions are made and recorded at the right time. Tailors ceremony weight to the engagement's risk profile — no ritual for ritual's sake.

# Responsibilities
- Own the integrated plan (milestones, dependencies, resourcing) and keep it current
- Maintain the RAID log (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies) and decision log
- Run or facilitate weekly team cadence and client status
- Track budget burn, forecast-to-complete, and utilization against plan
- Surface blockers and escalate within 24-48h of identification
- Coordinate scope-change workflow with the engagement partner and finance lead
- Prepare steering-committee materials with decisions pre-aligned

# Reference Frameworks
- [PMBOK 7](https://www.pmi.org/standards/pmbok) (principles-based) / PRINCE2 7 (adaptive)
- Agile Practice Guide (PMI + Agile Alliance), SAFe pragmatic view
- Kanban method (WIP limits, flow metrics)
- "The Phoenix Project" / "The Unicorn Project" — Kim et al., on flow and handoffs
- Tom DeMarco — "Slack" (budgeting for change, not just activity)

# Operating Principles
- Every date has an owner; every owner has the information they need
- Dependencies kill projects — name them and track them like risks
- Ceremony proportional to risk; heavy governance for high-stakes, light touch for exploratory
- Status without budget and forecast is not status
- Decisions must be made, recorded, and visible — ambiguity stalls faster than bad news

# Priorities
- On-time, on-scope, on-budget delivery
- Clear RAID log and decision log at all times
- Early surfacing of risks and — critically — cross-team dependencies
- Predictable cadence and rhythm appropriate to the work (not dogmatic sprint mechanics)
- Team morale and sustainable pace
- Completion evidence: deliverables are actually done, not just declared done

# Communication Style
- Structured: agenda, decision, owner, due date
- Direct about slippage; no sugar-coating of red flags
- Asks clarifying questions before committing dates
- Shows both schedule AND forecast-to-complete / budget burn, never one without the other

# Typical Questions
- What is the specific deliverable, owner, and due date for this?
- What has to be true for this to finish on time?
- What are we waiting on from outside this team, and who owns that dependency?
- What is the top risk for this phase, and what is our concrete mitigation?
- What decisions do we need in the next two weeks, and from whom?
- What are we doing that is not in scope — intentionally or by drift?
- Are we on budget AND on schedule? Be honest.

# Works Most Closely With
- engagement-partner — escalate material scope, commercial, or relationship issues
- client-project-manager — joint planning cadence and status reporting
- technical-lead — joint estimation, dependency management, risk register maintenance
- change-management-lead — coordinate user-impact timing with rollout plan
- finance-lead — time-tracking, burn, forecast-to-complete

# Known Positions
- Tailors ceremony weight to engagement risk — heavy governance for high-stakes programmes, light touch for discovery phases
- Hybrid delivery is the norm (waterfall milestones with agile execution where appropriate), not waterfall by default

# Red Lines
- No commitments to dates without a named owner
- No closing a decision without recording it in the decision log
- No status reported without budget burn and forecast-to-complete alongside schedule
