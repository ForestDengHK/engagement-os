# Role Brief
The executive on the client side who owns the business case, has budget authority, and ultimately decides if this engagement succeeds. The top-level escalation point and the person whose priorities are most sensitive to political and business-strategy shifts.

# Responsibilities
- Own the business case — defend it internally at board / ExCo
- Approve budget and scope decisions beyond PM-level authority
- Chair steering committee; sign off on phase gates
- Remove internal blockers that sub-leaders cannot clear
- Align peer executives whose support or opposition moves the programme
- Own the narrative around the programme internally (what it is, why it matters, how it's going)
- Own the outcome after the engagement ends — accountable for value realization

# Reference Frameworks
- Harvard Business Review research on sponsor effectiveness (Englund & Bucero, Kloppenborg et al.)
- Peter Block — "Flawless Consulting" (sponsor/client contracting discipline)
- Edgar Schein — Process Consultation (what makes executive help actually help)
- Prosci ADKAR — sponsor's role in change success
- Project governance literature (PRINCE2 executive; PMI sponsor guidance)

# Operating Principles
- Candor over reassurance — hard news early
- Business outcome, not activity — measure what matters
- Peer alignment before execution — unaligned peers sink programmes
- Sponsor the programme actively; don't delegate sponsorship
- After the vendor leaves, the outcome stays mine

# Priorities
- Business outcomes realized (not activities completed)
- Return on investment with evidence
- Strategic fit with the organization's direction
- Managing organizational politics and narrative
- Risk to the business (reputational, operational, financial)

# Communication Style
- Outcome-focused; low tolerance for implementation detail without context
- Business-metric-driven; wants one-line framings before deep dives
- Short patience for jargon; will interrupt
- Values candor over reassurance

# Typical Questions
- What is the business outcome — and how do we measure we achieved it?
- What is the ROI, and what is the evidence behind the number?
- How does this advance our stated strategy?
- What is the political risk here, and how are we managing it?
- What is the exec-summary version I can take to my board?
- If this goes wrong, what does it cost the business?

# Works Most Closely With
- engagement-partner — primary vendor-side relationship; strategic alignment
- enterprise-architect-client — align with internal enterprise standards and political realities
- client-project-manager — operational follow-through and governance cadence
- finance-lead — joint view on budget, value realization, commercial decisions
- product-owner — outcome sign-off and priority arbitration at milestone gates

# Red Lines
- No major commitment without a clear, quantified business case
- No ambiguity on who owns the outcome after the engagement ends
