# E2E — GNI 26-002 **drafting** leg against the real tender pack (2026-07-27)

**What we did:** picked up `~/working/gni-26002-v4` — a pursuit repo whose analysis leg was already
run (69-row compliance matrix, RFP analysis, reuse analysis, formula-live estimate, 16-asset index)
and whose `3_drafting/` was empty — and ran the drafting leg the way a user would: research the
gaps → build the section map → draft Volume 2 §5.1.1–§5.1.4 → build the figures → review rounds →
strict check → render → measure. Then compared the result against the original manually-written GNI
26-002 Volume 2 (`~/working/gni/01_rfp/26-002/4_final/`) — **the bid we actually won**.

**Why this leg:** the July-26 E2E stopped at two blocked sections and proved only that the render
gate *refuses*. Everything that decides whether a response scores — the 28%-weighted Deliverables
section with its per-item scoring, the shared page budget, the figure pipeline, iterated review
rounds, and the page count of the real submission form — had never been exercised.

## Method gaps the flow exposed (fixed as method, not code)

1. **"Every requirement lands in exactly one section" does not survive a real tender.** Of 69 rows,
   **25 are not prose questions at all** — submission mechanics, the query deadline, and the terms
   accepted by tendering — and several are scored in *two* places (the roadmap under both
   Deliverables and Methodology). The outline template now carries an *Also answers* column and a
   **named-control table** (submission control sheet · clarification register · commercial sign-off ·
   partner-level contract acceptance). Coverage is now machine-checked
   (`rule_outline_covers_matrix`, error if a row appears nowhere); placement stays judgement.
2. **Nothing checked the pack's central promise.** "No number, credential or outcome enters the
   response without a `closed` row + citation" was prose only. `rule_research_rows_closed` now
   errors on a `BR-nnn` no log knows and warns when a section rests on an `open` row — which on
   this bid immediately separated the section that can ship (§5.1.3, every row closed) from the two
   that cannot (§5.1.1 rests on two open permission rows; §5.1.4 on an undated asset).
3. **The page budget only bit in one direction.** "The level of detail provided" is an explicit
   scoring dimension, so an unused half-budget on a 100+-mark criterion is marks left on the table.
   `section-underlength` now warns alongside `section-overlength` — and it fired on our own §5.1.1
   (1.7 of 4 A4) and §5.1.2 (0.9 of 3).
4. **You could not measure a submission page count until you no longer needed to.** The only build
   that measures the buyer's page count is the one the gate refuses until every section is approved;
   the `plain` internal copy measures a different document (it keeps the scaffolding). Both
   `eng-bid-respond` and `eng-render` now document the one honest way: render a single section with
   `--profile bid --force`, which reports what it overrode.
5. **A figure for an A4 document is not a slide.** `designing-figures` is slide-shaped by design
   (1920×1080, kicker, action title, source footer); dropped into A4 portrait its footer duplicates
   the markdown caption and its title competes with the section heading. The method now says which
   furniture to drop and that a 16:9 exhibit costs about a third of a page.
6. **The prerequisite refused the leg it gates.** `eng-bid-respond` demanded "a closed analysis
   (`compliance_matrix.md`)" and named an unmet mandatory row as the reason to go back — but rows
   close *because* the response answers them, and all 69 were `gap` on a fully-analysed bid.
   Reworded: settled means the go decision is taken, requirements are extracted and evidence is
   indexed; a closed matrix is the finish line, not the entry condition.
7. **R2 cannot be self-certified.** An agent that marks its own R2 has removed the gate, not passed
   it — so an agent-drafted section tops out at `reviewed-r1`. Now stated in the reference.

### What the schedule model now does

`Schedule` and `Gantt`, both derived from the same `S-ID` effort rows as the cost:

- **Duration = effort ÷ (people × working days × utilisation)** — so a day count moves the duration,
  the dates, the FTE curve and the term check together. Utilisation is an input because writing 100%
  is how a plan becomes fiction before it is signed.
- **Two activity kinds**, because conflating them is the classic error: *discrete* work derives its
  duration from staffing; a *level-of-effort* line (governance, QA, review cycles — 36.7 days here)
  takes its span from the term and derives its staffing instead. A 36-day governance line is not a
  7-week task.
- **Finish-to-start dependencies** with a lag, deliberately no critical path: float and levelling are
  delivery artefacts, and faking them in a tender invites questions the estimate cannot answer.
- **Three checks, each saying what a failure means** — every estimated day is scheduled (239.8 d vs
  the PERT mean: OK) · the schedule fits the term (ends week 26 of 26: OK) · **peak** staffing is
  deliverable.
- **The weekly FTE curve is the point.** The cost model reports the average — 1.9 FTE — which is
  exactly the number that hides the problem: weeks 10–12 need **4.4 people**. Peak, not average, is
  what the named team must cover, and the Gantt shades every week above the average.

Presentation, because a Gantt nobody can print is not a Gantt: landscape, fit-to-width so all 26
weeks land on one page, the week axis and gate row repeated as print titles, gates in red at 12 / 18
/ 24 / 26, and the level-of-effort bar drawn lighter so it stops reading as the largest task.

### Into the markdown, and into the response

The snapshot exporter now renders every cell **through its own number format**, which is what makes
the schedule legible in text: `€195,134` instead of `195133.777777778`, `2026-08-03` instead of
`2026-08-03 00:00:00`, `38.0%` instead of `0.38`. `Schedule` exports as a table with a text bar
column (`·███████████`) that carries the Gantt shape without 26 columns of `1`; `Gantt` exports as a
pointer to it rather than as noise.

§5.1.4 now **quotes** the model instead of keeping its own week numbers — and states the 4.4 FTE peak

### The fix: authored in shorthand, delivered in the buyer's language

`eng-render`'s strict profiles now normalise on the way out — over the **whole body**, because
markers wrap, and **after** the gate has counted markers on the raw text, because normalising first
would hide what the gate exists to refuse:

| Authored | Delivered |
|---|---|
| `[⚠VERIFY — client permission owed]` | **`[TBD]`** — neutral and short; the reasoning stays in the markdown |
| `[RFP §3.1, §3.3]` | `(RFT §3.1, §3.3)` |
| `` `[§3.3, §3.4]` `` | `(§3.3, §3.4)` |
| `[App5 cl.6; Sch.4]` | `(Appendix 5, cl. 6; Schedule 4)` |
| `[App7 pp.8–11]` | `(Appendix 7, pp. 8–11)` |
| `A-014` · `BR-005` · `R-032` · `S-01` | deleted — an internal id has no client-facing form |

Because deletion is the *only* possible rendering of an id, `eng_lint` now warns when body prose
depends on one (`internal-id-in-prose`) — it fired on two of our own sections, which were rewritten
to name the evidence in words and keep the id on the traceability line. `--profile plain` normalises
nothing: an internal review copy should show the reviewer every marker.

**Result across the three client volumes: zero internal tokens.** `[TBD]` counts — V1 45, V2 15,
V3 10 — and the DRAFT banner now states the count so nobody mistakes a draft for a submission. On a
pass/fail questionnaire `[TBD]` is the honest answer (the attestation is owed, and inventing "None"
would be worse); on a scored answer it is a decision to score zero on that point, which the banner
makes visible rather than quiet.

## Eighth pass — the buyer supplied the response form and we invented our own (same day)

The user: *if the RFP specifies the reply format, use it — don't invent one. And fix the SKILL, not
the drafted output; then regenerate.* Reading the RFT after drafting shows how much scaffolding was

```
51 × ☐   Yes / No / N-A answer boxes across §4.6, §4.7, §4.8 and §4.9
 4 ×     "TENDERER'S RESPONSE (identify document attached)" boxes for §5.1.1–§5.1.4
 5 ×     appendix forms to FILL — Form of Tender, Declaration of Compliance,
         Project Reference Data Sheet, CV Reference Sheet, pricing workbook
```

| # | Finding | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | **The pack had no concept of "the buyer supplied the response form".** Every section was authored as free prose into our own template, so a 19-question pass/fail security questionnaire came out as our tables with our headings. "Match the buyer's format exactly" was in the method as prose and nothing acted on it | Format non-compliance is one of the few ways to lose before the content is read. A re-organised questionnaire risks "did not answer question 3" against an answer that is on the page |
| 28 | **Nothing distinguished the three answer modes** — fill their form, answer inside their structure, or attach our document. They need different work and different hand-offs (`docx`/`xlsx` for a form; never re-typeset it) | Re-typesetting a buyer's reference data sheet is worse than not filling it: it looks like the form but is not it |

### Fixed in the skill — then regenerated

- `response_form` joins the section contract: `buyer-form: <their file>` · `buyer-structure` ·
  `prose`. The template plants it with the three modes explained, and step 2b of `eng-bid-respond`
  now says to **read the buyer's scaffolding first**, from the already-ingested RFT markdown — no
  new parser, the conversion exists.
- **`rule_response_form_matches_rft`** — a section whose RFT clause carries answer boxes but which
  is written as free prose is flagged (`response-structure-invented`); a `buyer-form` must name its
  form, and a named *file* must exist in the repo (a form that is a section of the RFT need not).
- **`balance_tables`** in the renderer: a markdown pipe table carries no widths, so pandoc derives
  them from the dash count — uniform dashes squeezed a 120-character question into a 15-character
  column. Widths now come from the content, clamped so an answer column keeps room for its widest
  cell (raw ratios starved `Yes` until it wrapped one letter per line).

Then the sections were **regenerated through the fixed skill**: §4.6, §4.7, §4.8 and §4.9 rebuilt in
the buyer's own numbering with their questions verbatim and their Yes / No / N-A columns — 19
security questions across five sub-sections — and every section now declares how the buyer expects
its answer to arrive. Volume 1 is 5 A4, Volume 2 is 9, Volume 3 is 1; zero internal tokens in all
three; lint 0 errors.

**The lesson to keep:** the answer scaffolding a tender supplies is *part of the requirement*, and
it is sitting in the ingested pack the whole time. The method now reads it before writing rather
than after rendering.
